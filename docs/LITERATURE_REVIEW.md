# Literature review (verified)

Every paper below was checked against its publisher page, arXiv record or the PDF in the project folder (October 2026). The last column says what MOFChat takes from the paper or how it addresses the gap; "Limitation" and "Gap" are our reading of each paper.

| # | Paper | Authors | Year / venue | Method | Contribution | Limitation (our reading) | How MOFChat uses / addresses it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ChatMOF: an artificial intelligence system for predicting and generating metal-organic frameworks using large language models | Y. Kang, J. Kim | 2024, *Nature Communications* 15, 4705 | GPT-4 agent that plans and calls tools: table search with LLM-written pandas code, MOFTransformer property prediction, genetic-algorithm generation | First LLM agent for MOF search, prediction and generation; 96.9 % / 95.7 % / 87.5 % accuracy on search / prediction / generation | Token-limit failures on large tables; logic errors in generated code; online, proprietary LLM; final answers are not checked against the data | **Base paper.** MOFChat uses the same 12,020-MOF table, replaces LLM-written code with a validated JSON plan and parameterised SQL, runs locally on Llama 3 and validates the final answer |
| 2 | Revisiting Deep Learning Models for Tabular Data | Y. Gorishniy, I. Rubachev, V. Khrulkov, A. Babenko | 2021, NeurIPS | Feature Tokenizer + Transformer (FT-Transformer) benchmarked against MLP/ResNet/GBDT | Strong, simple deep baseline for tabular data | Quadratic attention cost in the number of features; GBDT still wins on some datasets | MOFChat's FT-Transformer (numeric + categorical tokens, [CLS], pre-norm blocks) follows this paper |
| 3 | Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on typical tabular data? | L. Grinsztajn, E. Oyallon, G. Varoquaux | 2022, NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks | Benchmark of trees vs. neural networks on ~10k-row tabular datasets | Trees remain state-of-the-art on medium-sized tabular data; explains why (irregular targets, uninformative features) | Benchmark datasets are generic, not materials | Motivates comparing the FT-Transformer against XGBoost; in MOFChat the two tie on surface area (R² 0.9815 vs 0.9818) |
| 4 | A multi-modal pre-training transformer for universal transfer learning in metal–organic frameworks (MOFTransformer) | Y. Kang, H. Park, B. Smit, J. Kim | 2023, *Nature Machine Intelligence* 5, 309–318 | Transformer pre-trained on 1 M hypothetical MOFs using atom graphs and energy grids | Universal MOF property predictor used by ChatMOF | Needs the 3D structure files and fine-tuned checkpoints for every property | MOFChat predicts from tabular descriptors instead (no structure files needed) and reports calibrated intervals |
| 5 | Advances, updates, and analytics for the computation-ready, experimental metal–organic framework database: CoRE MOF 2019 | Y. G. Chung et al. | 2019, *J. Chem. Eng. Data* 64, 5985–5998 | Curated, simulation-ready experimental MOF structures with Zeo++ geometric descriptors | The dataset behind ChatMOF's look-up table and MOFChat | Geometry only, no adsorption data | MOFChat's database (12,020 MOFs); ETL verifies duplicates, derived columns and the 3.31 Å probe rule |
| 6 | Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks | P. Lewis et al. | 2020, NeurIPS | Combine a dense retriever with a generator | Defines RAG | Retrieves text passages, not structured rows | MOFChat retrieves structured rows with SQL and text knowledge with dense retrieval, then generates |
| 7 | TableRAG: Million-Token Table Understanding with Language Models | S.-A. Chen, L. Miculicich, J. M. Eisenschlos, et al. | 2024, NeurIPS | Query expansion with schema and cell retrieval, so the LLM sees only the relevant parts of huge tables | Scales table QA to million-token tables | Generic tables; numeric constraints still evaluated by the LLM | MOFChat evaluates numeric constraints exactly in SQL; the LLM never filters rows |
| 8 | Chain-of-Table: Evolving Tables in the Reasoning Chain for Table Understanding | Z. Wang, H. Zhang, C.-L. Li, et al. | 2024, ICLR | LLM plans table operations step by step | State of the art on WikiTQ, FeTaQA and TabFact | Operations are still executed from LLM plans without domain validation | MOFChat also plans operations, but adds a domain validator (units, ranges, grounding) and self-correction |
| 9 | TARGET: Benchmarking Table Retrieval for Generative Tasks | X. Ji, P. Glenn, A. G. Parameswaran, M. Hulsebos | 2024, NeurIPS Table Representation Learning workshop | Benchmark of table retrievers for QA, fact verification and text-to-SQL | Dense retrievers beat BM25 but vary widely across tasks | Generic domains | MOFChat's labelled benchmark (64 + 20 questions) evaluates retrieval in the materials domain |
| 10 | Tabular Embedding Model (TEM): Finetuning Embedding Models for Tabular RAG Applications | S. Khanna, S. Subedi | 2024, arXiv 2405.01585 | Fine-tunes a small embedding model for tabular RAG (finance) | Domain-tuned small embedders beat general ones | Embeddings of numbers are poor at exact numeric constraints | Consistent with our finding: an untrained text embedding cannot satisfy numeric constraints (review-1 baseline, 9.4 % exact) |
| 11 | ChatGPT Chemistry Assistant for Text Mining and the Prediction of MOF Synthesis | Z. Zheng, O. Zhang, C. Borgs, J. T. Chayes, O. M. Yaghi | 2023, *J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 145, 18048–18062 | Prompt-engineered ChatGPT extracts synthesis conditions; ML predicts crystallization | 26,257 synthesis parameters for about 800 MOFs; prompts that reduce hallucination | Relies on a proprietary LLM | MOFChat's answer validator is a programmatic version of the "ground the LLM in data" idea |
| 12 | MOF-ChemUnity: Literature-Informed Large Language Models for Metal–Organic Framework Research | T. M. Pruyn, A. Aswad, S. T. Khan, J. Huang, R. Black, S. M. Moosavi | 2025, *J. Am. Chem. Soc.* | Multi-agent LLM workflow that links literature names to CSD structures in a knowledge graph | Literature-grounded MOF assistant with improved accuracy over plain LLMs | Large infrastructure; focuses on literature | MOFChat grounds answers in computed descriptors and adds literature through RAG (ChatMOF paper + curated notes) |
| 13 | L²M³OF: A Large Language Multimodal Model for Metal-Organic Frameworks | J. Cui, F. Wu, H. Zhao, et al. | 2025, arXiv 2510.20976 | Crystal-structure encoder projected into an LLM | Beats text-only LLMs on MOF property prediction | Preprint; needs structure files and fine-tuning | MOFChat stays tabular and lightweight; a multimodal encoder is listed as future work |
| 14 | A fast and elitist multiobjective genetic algorithm: NSGA-II | K. Deb, A. Pratap, S. Agarwal, T. Meyarivan | 2002, *IEEE Trans. Evol. Comput.* 6, 182–197 | Fast non-dominated sorting + crowding distance | Standard multi-objective selection operator | Designed for searching a continuous space | MOFChat applies NSGA-II selection to rank existing MOFs on several objectives (exact O(n log n) front sweep for two objectives) |
| 15 | A Unified Approach to Interpreting Model Predictions (SHAP) | S. M. Lundberg, S.-I. Lee | 2017, NeurIPS | Shapley-value feature attributions | Consistent, local explanations for any model | Explains the model, not causality | MOFChat explains every surface-area / void-fraction prediction with TreeSHAP |

Supporting methods used but not tabulated: Sentence-BERT (Reimers & Gurevych, EMNLP 2019) for text embeddings; FAISS (Johnson, Douze & Jégou, *IEEE Trans. Big Data* 2021) for vector search; Zeo++ (Willems et al., *Microporous Mesoporous Mater.* 149, 134 (2012)) for the dataset's descriptors; split-conformal prediction for calibrated intervals; Gaussian process regression (course lecture 5).
